My story

I didn’t start as an artist

I studied engineering. I had a career, a family, a full life. Art wasn’t part of the picture — at least not yet.

That changed one birthday, when someone gave me the gift of an art class. I walked in as an engineer with no art background. I walked out with something I hadn’t expected: a quiet, deep knowing that this was mine.

That was December 2017. Since then I haven’t stopped. I have been learning Indian folk art traditions one at a time — going deep, studying with masters, practicing until each form feels like a conversation rather than a technique. I am now an advanced student of Phad painting and I keep following the thread wherever it leads.

Today I am a practicing artist, a trained art docent, an art teacher, and a curriculum designer. I teach adults and kids in Maple Valley, WA. I take art into school classrooms as a docent. I design lesson plans that reach people who never thought art was “for them.”

That is exactly the point — art is for everyone. Yes, even you. Yes, even if you’ve never drawn a straight line in your life. That’s not a barrier. That’s the beginning.

What I practice

Six folk art traditions

Each tradition I study, I study deeply — with teachers from the lineage, with history in hand, with patience and reverence.

Madhubani

From Bihar, India · 2,500+ years old · Painted by women, passed mother to daughter

The artform I learned first was Madhubani — because when one day I read about it online, something in me went still. The story moved me deeply.

Long ago, King Janaka of Mithila — father of Sita — asked the women of his kingdom to paint the city with auspicious symbols to bless his daughter before her wedding. The women painted walls, floors, and doorways with every blessing they knew how to make with their hands. Those paintings are what we now call Madhubani, or Mithila painting.

This art was born from a father’s love for his daughter. It was carried forward by women, passed from mother to daughter for generations, in an unbroken matrilineal tradition. I learned from Vidushini Prasad, who learned from her mother, who is part of that same tradition.

When you learn Madhubani, you are not just learning a painting style. You are picking up a thread of blessing that has been passed through hands and hearts for thousands of years. You are making those blessings live on, through you, into the future.

If this story calls you or moves your heart — contact me. I will help you learn.

Phad

From Rajasthan, India · 700+ years old · Epic scroll painting tradition of the Bhopa community

Phad is one of the oldest scroll painting traditions in India. A single Phad scroll can be 30 to 45 feet long — a painted narrative of the entire life of a folk deity, used as a moving altar by the Bhopa storytellers who carry and perform with it.

I am studying Phad at an advanced level. Every session reminds me how much there is to learn. The detail, the discipline, the layers of story within story — it is a tradition that keeps giving the more you give to it.

Kalamkari

From Andhra Pradesh, India · 3,000+ years old · Hand-painted cotton cloth using a bamboo pen

Kalamkari means “work of the pen” — kalam (pen) + kari (work). Stories from epics like the Ramayana and Mahabharata are drawn and painted on fabric using a bamboo or palm pen dipped in natural dyes. Every line is drawn by hand, every color applied with intention.

It is one of the most meditative folk arts I practice. The slow, deliberate line work brings a stillness that is deeply centering.

Pattachitra

From Odisha, India · 12th century · Intricate cloth paintings of the Jagannath tradition

Pattachitra (patta = cloth, chitra = picture) is an Odisha tradition tightly woven with the Jagannath temple culture of Puri. The paintings are rich, detailed, and precise — figures outlined in bold black, filled with earthy natural pigments, framed by stylized leaf borders.

What strikes me most about Pattachitra is its completeness: every painting has a story, a rhythm, a border, a balance. Nothing is incidental.

Pichwai

From Rajasthan, India · 400+ years old · Devotional paintings hung behind the idol of Shrinathji

Pichwai paintings were created to be hung as sacred backdrops behind the idol of Shrinathji in the Nathdwara temple. The word itself means “hung at the back.” These are devotional works — rich with lotus flowers, cows, gopis, and the leelas of Lord Krishna.

Pichwai is the most intricate and meditative of the traditions I practice. The lotus motifs alone are a world of their own.

Gond

From Madhya Pradesh, India · Ancient tribal tradition · Every living thing filled with pattern and pulse

Gond art comes from the Gondi tribal community of central India. It is one of the most joyful folk art traditions I know — animals, trees, and people rendered in swirling patterns of dots and lines, as though everything in nature is vibrating with its own inner energy.

Children take to Gond immediately. The patterns are playful, the animals are expressive, and there are no rigid rules — only the invitation to fill every shape with life.

The journey

How it unfolded

Dec 2017

A birthday gift changes everything

Gifted an art class. Walked in as an engineer. Found something I didn’t know I was looking for.

2018

First steps in Madhubani

Began studying with Vidushini Prasad. Learned the origin story. Understood for the first time what it means to carry a tradition.

2019

Going deeper — more traditions

Added Kalamkari, Pichwai, and Pattachitra to my practice. Each one opened a different door.

2020

Becoming a teacher

Started teaching others — first informally, then with intention. Realized that teaching makes you learn differently and more deeply.

2022

Art docent training

Trained as an art docent to bring art into school classrooms. Met children who had never held a paintbrush. Saw what happened when they did.

2024

Advanced Phad & Shakthi Circle

Enrolled in an advanced Phad painting course. Started Shakthi Circle on WhatsApp to share monthly skills with women of all ages.

2025–2026

Classes in Maple Valley & what’s next

Running Indian folk art classes and workshops in Maple Valley, WA. Currently drawn toward Chola mural art — planning to study it properly and create artwork to truly understand the era and its artists.
